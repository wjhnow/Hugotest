# Your Eyes Are Lying to You (Kind Of)

Here's a weird one for you.

The image that actually lands at the back of your eye is upside down. Fully flipped. Top is bottom. Left is right. And yet, right now, the world in front of you looks completely normal. Nothing's floating on its head.

So what's going on?

## Light comes in, flips, and lands wrong

Your pupil lets light in. Your cornea and lens do the same job a camera lens does — they bend that light and focus it onto your retina, at the back of the eye.

But that's the catch with lenses: the image they focus ends up inverted. Upside down, mirrored. It's not a defect. It's just optics. Cameras and projectors do the exact same thing.

## Your brain quietly cleans it up

The retina doesn't know or care that the picture's flipped. It just converts light into electrical signals and fires them down the optic nerve toward the brain. Your visual cortex is the one that receives this upside-down data — and somewhere in there, it gets sorted out. You never consciously see the inverted version. It happens so fast, so automatically, that it feels like the world was really never flipped in the first instance.
